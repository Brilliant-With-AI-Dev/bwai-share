How to use

A starting point for a better conversation

This map brings together leadership perspectives on Edelman APAC’s priorities and AI-enabled change.

Start with the patterns

Start with the overview. Explore shared ground, differing perspectives, and choices that remain open. Use these patterns to decide where further conversation would help.

Common Ground highlights broad overlap in perspectives and approaches.

Partial Alignment highlights varied perspectives, with broadly similar approaches.

Divergent Perspectives highlights different approaches or priorities, whether perspectives overlap or differ.

Symbols summarize patterns
Aligned POV and Approach
Varied POVs, Similar Approaches
Similar POV, Divergent Approaches
Divergent POVs and Approaches

Read the relationship

Each symbol brings together how leaders understand an issue and how they suggest responding.

Read its caption for the specific shared ground or difference. Similar does not mean identical; divergent approaches may still work together.

These relationships can overlap. Each topic highlights the pattern most useful for understanding its unresolved decision.

Three layers of insight

What leaders said: perspectives drawn from the interviews, plus Blind Spots and Moonshots developed from those signals.

Between the lines: connections inferred from interviews, with any supporting market context identified.

Strategic Provocation: wider agency and AI developments, and questions about what they could mean for APAC.

Follow the evidence

Open Leader perspectives & evidence to explore individual readings and source excerpts. Close the panel to return to the same place in the map.

Each role’s reading and qualification appear immediately. Open its reasoning and source excerpts for more context.

Make the interpretation better

These are interpretations of AI-assisted conversations, not definitive judgments or agreed commitments. Human reviewers have not confirmed every interpretation against its full context.

If something misrepresents a view, correct it. A useful clarification strengthens the discussion.

02 · Between the lines

Connections worth testing

These hypotheses connect interview signals with relevant external research. Each shows the starting observation, the possible implication, and a question to explore.

Productivity gains may change where revenue is credited

Interview signal

The interviews connect leaner delivery with shared-account proration: revenue divided between markets using relative hours and charge-out rates.

Possible implication

Fewer weighted hours could reduce one market’s share of an unchanged fee. Lower costs or redeployed capacity could offset that effect.

External context

Consulting research shows task-level productivity gains, but does not establish their effect on account economics.

Applies only where that allocation method operates. The CFO describes APAC as more self-contained than EMEA. No AI-caused revenue loss is established.

Explore

On one shared account, how would fewer hours change revenue credit and actual costs?

HBS: Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier

Time saved needs a destination

Interview signal

The interviews combine proactive client service with clearer scope, planning, and negotiation before discounting.

Possible implication

Additional requests could absorb released capacity. Deliberate reinvestment could instead strengthen retention, quality, or paid work.

External context

Task-level productivity research does not show whether saved time produces account profit.

Additional service can be valuable. This is a question about how capacity is used, not an assumption that extra work is wasted.

Explore

Which pilot can track saved hours, extra requests, fees, quality, and client response?

HBS: Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier

Automating tasks changes how judgment develops

Interview signal

The interviews express ambitions for stronger client leaders, more thinking, and less routine work.

Possible implication

Automation could remove useful practice or create better learning opportunities. Workflow design and supervision will shape the result.

External context

Customer-support research found learning benefits from AI assistance. It does not establish how communications judgment develops.

No learning decline is established here. Which tasks develop judgment remains a question to examine.

Explore

Which judgment skill should one redesigned workflow develop, and how will we assess it?

Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond: Generative AI at Work

Participation may depend on the employee bargain

Interview signal

The interviews describe workload concerns, limited experimentation time, and constraints on locally determined rewards.

Possible implication

Clear expectations about workload, learning, and support could make participation easier. Skills and workflow barriers also matter.

External context

OECD research associates training and worker consultation with better reported outcomes. It does not demonstrate causation.

The connection between rewards and AI participation is inferred. The interviews also anticipate uptake where new processes prove useful.

Explore

What can leadership credibly promise about workload, learning time, and support during this pilot?

OECD: AI surveys of employers and workers

Visible assurance could strengthen the offer

Interview signal

The interviews link early research checks and quality standards with ambitions for stronger talent and higher fees.

Possible implication

Evidence trails and expert review could help clients assess quality. Whether they justify extra fees depends on what clients already expect.

External context

WFA reports buyer interest in AI transparency. NIST recommends source verification. Neither establishes willingness to pay a premium.

Some workflows already include expert checks. The opportunity is to test useful additional assurance, not assume a missing capability.

Explore

Which client values additional verification, and what would exceed their existing quality expectations?

WFA and ICAS: AI labelling and transparency · NIST AI 600-1: Generative AI Profile

Realized gains could fund a visible employee benefit

Interview signal

The interviews combine aspirations for stronger talent and better rewards with pressure on time and resources.

Possible implication

Verified gains could fund development or rewards, provided the funding and decision authority are clear.

External context

OECD research supports examining employee experience, but does not test AI gain-sharing schemes.

This is a proposal to explore, not a leadership commitment. Released hours do not automatically create spendable cash.

Explore

After a pilot, which verified gain could fund which benefit, with whose authorization?

03 · Strategic Provocation

What will clients pay Edelman APAC to own as AI expands their alternatives?

AI is changing what clients can produce and how brands are discovered. The opportunity is to define what clients still need Edelman to deliver—and why it deserves investment.

WFA: How AI is changing in-house agencies · WFA: AI visibility and discoverability · Edelman: Decision-grade intelligence
01 · Value

Which client problems can we solve distinctively as production and discovery change?

Clients are building AI capabilities while discovery channels change. A distinctive offer needs a clear buyer, useful outcomes, and a reason to choose Edelman.

Explore
  • Which client decision improves materially because Edelman is involved?
  • Which buyer, budget, and alternative would each proposed offer address?
  • What evidence would make a client expand or retain our mandate?

02 · Economics

How will we turn AI productivity into profitable growth that clients also value?

Faster work changes capacity, costs, and client expectations. Profitable growth depends on how those gains enter the commercial model.
