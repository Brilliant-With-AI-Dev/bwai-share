Connections to test · authored hypotheses

What could these signals mean for Edelman APAC?

These hypotheses connect what the interviews imply with relevant agency and AI developments and first-principles reasoning. They suggest possibilities to explore; they do not establish effects or represent leadership agreement.

01 · Hypothesis

Could saving time reduce a market’s revenue credit?

Fewer weighted hours could reduce a market’s share of an unchanged fee. Costs and redeployment determine the financial effect.

For the room
  1. On one shared account, how would fewer hours change revenue credit and actual costs?
Basis and limitsInterview observations

The CFO describes shared-account revenue allocation using relative hours and charge-out rates. The CEO wants leaner delivery and less routine work.

Conditional hypothesis for qualifying shared accounts. The CFO says APAC work is more self-contained than EMEA. No observed AI-caused loss, universal penalty, or leader agreement is established.

Sources: HBS: Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier

02 · Hypothesis

Where will the time saved actually go?

Extra requests could absorb released capacity. Deliberate reinvestment could instead improve retention or generate paid work.

For the room
  1. Which pilot can track saved hours, extra requests, fees, quality, and client response?
Basis and limitsInterview observations

The CEO wants anticipated work and trusted client prioritization. The COO wants clearer scope and planning; the CFO favors negotiating scope before discounting.

Interview observations source-checked. Additional service may be valuable; task-level gains do not establish account profitability. No claim that all additional requests are unpriced or wasteful.

Sources: HBS: Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier

03 · Hypothesis

How will people learn judgment as routine work changes?

Automation could remove practice or create better learning opportunities. Supervision and responsibility will shape the result.

For the room
  1. Which judgment skill should one redesigned workflow develop, and how will we assess it?
Basis and limitsInterview observations

The CEO wants stronger client leaders and account executives doing more thinking. He also wants technology to handle routine work.

Interview observations source-checked. No decline in learning, or claim that particular routine tasks teach judgment, is established. Historical support-sector research suggests possible learning benefits.

Sources: NBER: Generative AI at Work

04 · Hypothesis

What should employees expect during an AI pilot?

Uncertainty about workload, support, or personal benefit may affect participation. Skills and workflow barriers may also matter.

For the room
  1. What can leadership credibly promise about workload, learning time, and support during this pilot?
Basis and limitsInterview observations

The CPO reports concerns about doing more with fewer people. The CEO describes understaffing, limited experimentation time, and global conditions constraining local bonuses.

Interview observations source-checked. Bonus constraints and AI concerns are separate observations; their connection is our hypothesis. The CFO expects uptake of useful processes, so hesitation cannot be generalized across teams.

Sources: OECD: AI surveys of employers and workers

05 · Hypothesis

Could visible verification strengthen a premium offer?

Evidence trails and expert review could help clients judge quality. Clients may expect that assurance within existing fees.

For the room
  1. Which client values additional verification, and what would exceed their existing quality expectations?
Basis and limitsInterview observations

The Chief of Staff proposes checking foundational research before it shapes complex campaigns. The COO emphasizes quality standards; the CEO wants stronger talent and higher fees.

Interview observations source-checked. Existing expert checks already protect some workflows. This commercial hypothesis establishes no separate demand, willingness to pay, or exclusive trust advantage.

Sources: WFA and ICAS: AI labelling and transparency · NIST AI 600-1: Generative AI Profile

06 · Hypothesis

Could verified gains fund a visible employee benefit?

Realized gains could fund development or rewards. Sustainable funding and decision authority would need to be explicit.

For the room
  1. After a pilot, which verified gain could fund which benefit, with whose authorization?
Basis and limitsInterview observations

The CEO wants better pay, stronger talent, and incentives reflecting local achievement. The CPO describes limited time and pressure to do more with less.

Interview observations source-checked. No benefit pool or AI gain-sharing mechanism is proposed by these leaders. Released hours are not automatically cash; OECD evidence does not test AI savings-sharing.

Sources: OECD: AI surveys of employers and workers

Strategic provocation · September 2026

What will clients pay Edelman APAC to own as AI expands their alternatives?

AI expands how clients produce work and find information. Edelman needs a valued mandate, sound economics, and reliable delivery. The questions test those choices without assuming every task or fee disappears.

Sources: WFA: How AI is changing in-house agencies · WFA: AI visibility and discoverability · WPP: 2026 interim results

01 · Subordinate question

Which client problems can we solve distinctively as production and discovery change?

Buyers are developing internal AI capabilities while discovery channels change. A durable offer needs a clear buyer, meaningful value, and evidence of differentiation.

Connects to: Recovery

For the room
  1. Which client decision improves materially because Edelman is involved?
  2. Which buyer, budget, and alternative would each proposed offer address?
  3. What evidence would make a client expand or retain our mandate?
Possible 90-day test

Choose two client problems and named pilot accounts. Validate each buyer’s alternatives and success criteria. Test a paid offer; record demand, delivery quality, and reasons for acceptance or rejection.

Landscape context and limits

In-house AI investment is growing, but integration remains uneven.

AI discovery creates coordination needs across owned information, publishers, creators, and communities.

Edelman Intelligence already describes strategic AI advisory and measurement capabilities. APAC can test their fit against specific client needs.

Unilever describes faster production; NIVEA emphasizes cultural understanding and differentiation.

Authored proposal for local review. External examples do not establish Edelman client demand.

Sources: WFA: How AI is changing in-house agencies · WFA: AI visibility and discoverability · NIVEA’s Tobias Collée: WFA interview · Unilever: Desire at Scale · Edelman: Decision-grade intelligence

02 · Subordinate question

How will we turn AI productivity into profitable growth that clients also value?

Faster tasks can change prices, scope, quality, and capacity simultaneously. Financial gains depend on actual costs, commercial terms, and reinvestment decisions.

Connects to: Recovery · Client work

For the room
  1. Where do faster workflows improve contribution after all delivery costs?
  2. How will saved capacity become client value, paid work, or lower costs?
  3. Which fee and allocation rules support that result?
Possible 90-day test

Choose one local account and, if relevant, one qualifying shared account. Establish comparable scope and quality baselines. Track fees, revenue credit, delivery costs, review, rework, and actual capacity use. Separate modeled scenarios from observed results. Agree what contribution and client evidence would justify changing the fee structure.

Landscape context and limits

Buyer expectations differ between strategic expertise and automated tasks.

WPP’s production growth alongside group decline cautions against universal collapse claims.

WFA’s 2024 buyers anticipated both rising total agency fees and cheaper AI-enabled tasks.

Internal allocation is distinct from customer pricing. No assumption that saved hours become cash or outcome fees repair economics.

Sources: WPP: 2026 interim results · WFA: Future of agency remuneration · HBS: Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier

03 · Subordinate question

What must APAC change, control, and develop to make that promise credible?

Reliable delivery needs clear authority, usable evidence, and people capable of assessing outputs. Local expertise and shared standards must work together.

Connects to: Quality · Capacity · Ownership · Regional adaptation

For the room
  1. Which decisions must APAC own, and which require agreement with Global?
  2. Who verifies source evidence before it shapes client work?
  3. How will the workflow develop judgment and protect learning time?
Possible 90-day test

Assign one pilot owner for quality and economics. Define local exceptions and escalation rights. Review deliverables across relevant languages; assess one judgment skill before and after supervised practice.

Landscape context and limits

Brands seek contextual transparency around AI-generated material.

Edelman’s regional leadership appointments coexist with its broader operating-model changes.

NIVEA’s executive describes developing concepts locally before translating them for headquarters.

APAC decision rights and interview interpretations require confirmation. Historical learning evidence does not predict inevitable skill loss.

Sources: WFA and ICAS: AI labelling and transparency · Edelman: EMEA and Middle East CEO appointments · NBER: Generative AI at Work · OECD: AI surveys of employers and workers · NIST AI 600-1: Generative AI Profile · NIVEA’s Tobias Collée: WFA interview

Research sources, dates, and limits

WFA: How AI is changing in-house agencies

2025-11-04

Research involving 30 global in-house leaders reported further AI investment plans and uneven integration. Ninety-three percent planned investment; 12% reported full integration.

Small selected sample; self-reported intentions and implementation. Historical 2025 findings, not verified 2026 APAC sourcing changes.

WFA: AI visibility and discoverability

2026-06-08

A 27-brand webinar sample reported concern about AI discovery and limited established ownership. Guidance recommends coordinating authoritative information across channels.

Small selected sample; guidance co-produced with DEPT. Neither proven service demand nor causal evidence that GEO investment increases sales.

WPP: 2026 interim results

2026-08-06

WPP describes an integrated operating model supported by its AI platform. H1 production grew 1.6% like-for-like while group revenue less pass-through costs fell 4.7%.

Company strategy and reported financial measures. Mixed business causes; no attribution of growth or decline to AI.

WFA: Future of agency remuneration

2024-11-14

In a survey of 80-plus multinationals, 61% expected total agency fees to rise. Fifty-eight percent expected lower fees where AI was deployed; 84% cited outcome-measurement barriers.

Historical intentions concerning media relationships. Not actual 2026 PR contracts. Lower task fees and higher overall spending can coexist.

WFA and ICAS: AI labelling and transparency

2026-04-02

Among 27 multinational brands, 82% considered AI transparency essential for brand reputation. Disclosure preferences varied by use case.

Small selected survey; attitudes, not measured trust effects or willingness to pay. Voluntary guidance is not law.

Edelman: EMEA and Middle East CEO appointments

2026-05-07

The announcement retains regional CEOs and describes combining market leadership, specialist expertise, and operations.

Confirms appointments and stated structure, not precise APAC authority. Does not support claims that regions were abolished.

HBS: Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier

2023-09-21

An experiment involving 758 consultants found faster completion and better performance within tested AI capabilities. Effects depended on the task.

Historical GPT-4 experiment, not a current-model benchmark or agency-margin study. Task productivity is not realized financial savings.

NBER: Generative AI at Work

2023

Customer-support field research found larger productivity gains for novices and suggestive evidence of learning.

Historical working paper, subsequently published in 2025. Sector-specific; assisted performance does not prove independent communications judgment. Primary search index reconfirmed the abstract; direct page retrieval returned 403.

OECD: AI surveys of employers and workers

2023-03-27

Worker surveys found positive experiences alongside job concerns. Training and worker consultation were associated with better reported outcomes.

Finance and manufacturing across seven countries. Associations are not causal effects. Predates widespread contemporary generative AI use; does not test savings-sharing.

NIST AI 600-1: Generative AI Profile

2024-07

Section 2.2 identifies confabulation risks. Action MS-2.5-003 recommends verifying sources and citations during evaluation and ongoing monitoring.

Risk-management guidance, not evidence of Edelman quality failures, certification, or demand for paid assurance.

NIVEA’s Tobias Collée: WFA interview

2026-03-03

A named buyer emphasizes differentiation, trust, cultural understanding, and local-language concept development.

One executive’s views and retrospective experience. Not an endorsement of Edelman or evidence of a price premium.

Unilever: Desire at Scale

2025-09-30

Unilever describes internal studios and faster, culturally relevant production across several Asian markets.

Buyer self-report; supplier fees, full costs, and causal performance methods are unavailable. Does not establish external agency elimination.

Edelman: Decision-grade intelligence

2026-05-20

Edelman announced expanded strategic AI advisory, advanced data, and measurement capabilities through Edelman Intelligence.

Corporate description of an offering, not independent proof of demand, differentiation, or financial return.
