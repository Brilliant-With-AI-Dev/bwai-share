What should we protect, change, or invest in for profitable recovery?

These approaches can reinforce each other. The unresolved choice is their sequencing and resource tradeoffs.

The CFO prioritizes compensation control; the Chief of Staff resists margin dominating recovery. This is a priority tension, not cuts-versus-growth camps. Neither rejects financial discipline or growth.

What underpins these positions

C
CEO, APAC

Strengthen the integrated communications core and talent; win larger mandates while protecting people and client happiness.

Seek fewer, larger clients and integrated mandates, higher quality work, higher fees and better-paid talent. AI alone cannot solve the commercial and operating problems that determine profitable growth.

Applying these priorities to the workshop's 90-day profitable-recovery frame is a synthesis. The OpEx-versus-compensation comparison explicitly addressed 90 days; other priorities were discussed over longer horizons.

Her dashboard already exists. The need is consistent driver data, repeatable production and drill-down, not inventing a dashboard. AI assistance is conditional on trusted inputs and interpretation. No completed staffing reassignment or collective 90-day commitment is established.

Her desired win-win explicitly combines jobs, financial targets, collaboration, and transformation. Workday and talent acquisition are her concrete capability sequence, not a fully specified regional investment allocation.

Job preservation is a desired outcome, not a categorical rejection of cost savings or all headcount reductions. No recovery portfolio, pricing strategy, or investment budget is specified.
