Planning Mirror · proposed pilot flow

From private perspective to shared action.

Each stage turns broad context into a more specific planning asset. Each asset makes the next conversation sharper, safer, and more useful.

  1. 01Define the planning goal
  2. 02Shape the process privately
  3. 03Gather participant insight
  4. 04Turn insight into action

Initial-cycle support: Because this is the first Planning Mirror for GoTu, BWAI will play a more active role than in future cycles.

The rule beneath every stage

Trust comes before synthesis.

Each participant must feel free to share honestly. These principles govern the entire flow.

  1. No one ever sees another person's transcript or personal Mirror artifacts.
  2. Strong guardrails, LLM-judgment checkpoints, and participant-controlled sharing permissions ensure that no distilled notes or insights betray any participant's trust or confidence.
  3. Each participant approves, revises, or removes every LLM-derived insight/note before it's released for synthesis or sharing.
  4. If patterns emerge from de-identified, distilled insights, BWAI will review and determine whether and how to share with GoTu, to ensure that individual confidentiality is protected.
01 Set direction

Definition

Starting point · broad parameters + current materials

After a short call with BWAI to set broad parameters*, the GoTu planning lead onboards the Planning Mirror like a new team member, through current process documents and Mirror interviews.

Produces A planning brief—goals, roles, scope, reasons, named Process Shapers for Stage 2, and participant groups for Stage 3—plus LLM-optimized versions of current process documentation.

* We can't design useful interviews until we know who will have them and why. Documents describe a past process, not this one. ↩

02 Shape privately

Shaping

Private conversations

Uses · Stage 1 planning brief + documentation

The Process Shapers complete private Mirror conversations. Each conversation may draw on the person's own Mirror context, approved GoTu Mirror artifacts, and Stage 1 outputs.

Produces Private insights for each Shaper, plus distilled insights toward the shared plan.
2A Approve the runbook

Synthesis and approval

BWAI · human-led

Uses · Stage 1–2 outputs

BWAI reviews and combines the Stage 1–2 outputs into an approved planning runbook: the order and purpose of each participant round; prompts, goals, and outputs; and review, privacy, release, and decision rules.

This human-led step matters most with multiple Process Shapers. The product can automate more later, but not in the first cycle.

Produces The approved planning runbook that governs Stages 3–4.
03 Hear every voice

Participant conversations

Private conversations

Uses · Stage 2A approved runbook

Each Planning Participant named in the runbook completes a private Mirror conversation. It follows the runbook's prompts and goals, and may draw on the participant's own Mirror context.

Trust in practice: per , sharing starts on hold. Each participant approves, revises, or removes every distilled insight before release.

Produces A personal planning playbook (or whatever output the runbook defines), private insights for the participant, and approved distilled insights for the group process.

Rounds are possible. Leaders might go first; a later, larger group builds on approved, frozen results. The runbook defines each handoff before participant work begins.

3A Find shared patterns

Group synthesis

BWAI · human-led

Uses · Participant-released inputs only

After all required rounds finish, BWAI operates the initial group synthesis using only participant-released inputs.

Per the , the draft is LLM- and human-checked for identifying language, misstated views, over-merged themes, and participation below the agreed minimum. Potentially unsafe material stays out. Every edit is recorded.

Per , BWAI reviews whether non-attributable patterns can be shared without identifying individuals or small groups.

Produces A group planning readout (the main themes, tensions, risks, and open choices); a Planning Action runbook (decisions to make, proposed actions, and follow-up questions); and any other outputs the runbook agreed to, such as leadership or team insights.
04 Make the calls

Delivery and action

Uses · Stage 3A readout + action runbook

The Planning Mirror delivers the Stage 3A outputs through a workshop or async process, or both—whichever the runbook chose. (Whether BWAI offers facilitation is TBD.)

GoTu's leaders and teams rank priorities, revise proposals, make decisions, assign owners, and set dates. The product guides the work; GoTu's people make the calls.

Produces Decisions, actions, owners, dates, follow-up measures, and GoTu's decision to continue, revise, or stop the approach for the next cycle.
