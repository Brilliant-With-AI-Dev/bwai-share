Quarterly Planning Mirror · proposed pilot flow

From private perspective to shared action.

GoTu enters quarterly planning with candidate rocks, prioritized issues, and hard differences already clear—so the room can spend more time IDS-ing priorities, milestones, and decisions.

  1. 01Calibrate and onboard the Mirror
  2. 02Shape the planning cycle
  3. 03Prepare and run the offsite
  4. 04Carry outcomes into next quarter

Proposed two-quarter pilot: October 2026 and January 2027, then reassess for the rest of 2027. This is a sketch, not a commitment: GoTu's experience will determine how the flow and outputs evolve. Success means less manual synthesis for Tev and faster time-to-substance in the offsite.

The rule beneath every stage

Alignment requires truth. Truth demands trust.

Each participant must feel free to share honestly. These principles govern the entire flow.

  1. Personal transcripts and Individual Mirror artifacts stay private by default. Shared outputs omit names by default, aggregate patterns where possible, and include only participant-approved contributions.
  2. Safeguards reduce direct attribution and inference risk, but cannot eliminate inference.
  3. Each participant approves, revises, or removes every AI-generated insight before it can contribute to a Functional or Group output.
  4. BWAI supports pilot setup and timeboxed synthesis for the initial cycles only. GoTu owns the planning session, distribution decisions, and every business decision.
Pilot roles

Roles

  1. Lead — Tev. Runs the pilot and makes the final calls on scope and direction.
  2. Shapers — GoTu-selected participants who help shape the cycle. Cary, Ed, and Gerardo are recommended for this pilot; Tev retains final approval.
  3. Planners — the offsite participants, named by GoTu in Stage 03.
  4. GoTu — chooses participants, controls distribution, runs the offsite, and owns every decision.
Output-tier legend

Three kinds of output.

Every stage declares its outputs in three tiers:

  1. Individual (person-bound) — belongs to the person. Private by default and shared only with their approval. Includes a person's Mirror conversation and Mirror letter.
  2. Functional (seat-bound) — belongs to the role or task: whoever does this thing, now or in the future. It transfers with the seat, never the person, so accumulated context survives any handoff.
  3. Group (GoTu-wide) — belongs to GoTu collectively. Shared to the room; exportable.
How sharing works

A private conversation can inform a Functional or Group output only after the participant approves it. Distribution does not change ownership: the Planning Toolkit remains Functional when Shapers share it with the room; decisions and rocks are Group.

04 Personal planning view

Planning Mirrors

Mirror Interaction How60–90-minute conversational Mirror WhoPlanners UsesBrief + approved context + EOS pre-work

About ten days before the offsite, each Planner completes a conversational Mirror that replaces the one-off written questionnaire. Planners can talk freely through priorities, elephants, risks, dependencies, and proposed solutions. The Mirror follows important threads one meaningful step deeper, then helps distinguish shared priorities, distinct concerns, and issues whose emotional intensity may exceed their business weight.

05 Tools for planning

The Planning Toolkit

Mirror Artifact HowSynthesis of approved inputs WhoMirror + timeboxed BWAI support UsesParticipant-approved Mirror inputs

The Mirror performs first-pass compilation and synthesis so Tev can calibrate and exercise judgment instead of manually consolidating every submission. The readout shows counts or proportions for strong alignment and material splits, and flags when shared language masks different problems. The pilot target, when the inputs support it, is up to roughly 15 candidate rocks and 10–15 prioritized issues, prepared for GoTu to force-rank.

Contains
  • Pre-drafted rocks + proposed sub-items
  • Prioritized issues + elephants
  • Alignment + material splits
  • False alignment to resolve
  • Isolated or sustained signals to test
  • Recurring solution patterns
