GoTu planning flow

Each stage below turns broad context into a more specific planning asset. That asset informs the subsequent stage.

Note: Because this is the initial version of the Planning Mirror — both for GoTu and generally — BWAI team members will play a more active and direct role in the process than they will in future iterations.

Prioritizing Participant Trust

To ensure that each participant, at every point, feels free to share honestly, as a matter of principle and policy:

  1. No one ever sees another person's transcript or personal Mirror artifacts.
  2. Strong guardrails, LLM-judgment checkpoints, and participant-controlled sharing permissions ensure that no distilled notes or insights betray any participant's trust or confidence.
  3. Each participant approves, revises, or removes every LLM-derived insight/note before it's released for synthesis or sharing.
  4. If patterns emerge from de-identified, distilled insights, BWAI will review and determine whether and how to share with GoTu, to ensure that individual confidentiality is protected.

Stage 1: Definition

After a short call with BWAI to set some broad parameters*, the GoTu planning lead onboards the Planning Mirror like a new team member, through some mix of current process documents and Mirror interviews.

Produces a planning brief (goals, roles, scope, reasons — including the named Process Shapers for Stage 2 and the participant groups for Stage 3) and LLM-optimized versions of current process documentation.

* We can't design useful interviews until we know who will have them, and why; documents alone describe a past process, not this one. ↩

Stage 2: Shaping

Private conversations

Uses: Stage 1 planning brief + documentation

The Process Shapers complete private Mirror conversations. Each conversation may draw on the person's own Mirror context, approved GoTu Mirror artifacts, and the Stage 1 outputs.

Produces private insights for each Shaper, plus distilled insights toward the shared plan.

Stage 2A: Synthesis and approval

BWAI, human-led

Uses: Stage 1–2 outputs

BWAI reviews and combines the Stage 1–2 outputs into an approved planning runbook: the details, order and purpose of each participant round; the prompts, goals, and outputs for each conversation; and the review, privacy, release, and decision rules.

This human-led step matters most if there are multiple designated Process Shapers — the product can automate more of it later, but not in the first cycle.

Produces the approved planning runbook that governs Stages 3–4.

Stage 3: Participant conversations

Private conversations

Uses: Stage 2A approved runbook

Each Planning Participant named in the runbook completes a private Mirror conversation. Each conversation follows the runbook's prompts and goals, and may draw on the participant's own Mirror context (if available).

Prioritizing trust: per above, sharing starts on hold — each participant approves, revises, or removes every distilled insight before it's released for the group process.

Produces a personal planning playbook (or whatever output the runbook defines), private insights for the participant, and approved distilled insights for the group process.

Note: Cycles may run in rounds. Leaders might go first; a later, larger group builds on the approved, frozen results of the earlier round. The runbook will define these handoffs before participant work begins.

Stage 3A: Group synthesis

BWAI, human-led

Uses: Stage 3 participant-released inputs only

After all required rounds finish, BWAI operates the initial group synthesis, using only participant-released inputs.

Per the above, the draft is LLM- and human-checked for identifying language, misstated views, over-merged themes, and participation below the agreed minimum. Anything potentially unsafe stays out, and every exclusion and edit is recorded.

Note: per , if patterns emerge that are not individually attributable and can be shared without identifying individuals or small groups, BWAI will review and determine whether and how to share them with GoTu.

Produces a group planning readout (the main themes, tensions, risks, and open choices); a Planning Action runbook (decisions to make, proposed actions, follow-up questions); and any other outputs the runbook agreed to, such as leadership or team insights.

Stage 4: Delivery and action

Uses: Stage 3A readout + action runbook

The Planning Mirror delivers the Stage 3A outputs through a workshop or async process, or both — whichever the runbook chose. (Whether BWAI offers facilitation is TBD.)

GoTu's leaders and teams rank priorities, revise proposals, make decisions, assign owners, and set dates. The product guides the work; GoTu's people make the calls.

Produces decisions, actions, owners, dates, and follow-up measures — and GoTu's call on whether to continue, revise, or stop the approach for the next cycle.
