Looking back

  • Top of mind

    Honestly, what’s sitting on me is that we spent a whole quarter arguing about how we build with AI and never actually decided anything. I mean, we shipped, the numbers held, but every Monday was the same conversation again, and I walked into most weeks already tired of it.

  • Personal Best

    I took my daughter on the college tour I’d been putting off for two years. Four days, no laptop, and I didn’t open the fill-rate channel once.

  • Professional Best

    Getting sales and ops onto one rate card for the Gulf Coast markets. That had been stuck for three quarters and we landed it in about six weeks without anybody getting rolled, and the offices stopped hearing two different prices from us.

  • Quarter Rating

    Seven, maybe six and a half.

  • Rating Reason

    It was two quarters in one, honestly. On delivery we were good — fill rate held through the summer dip, the Gulf Coast rate card finally landed, engineering shipped the worker app rewrite more or less when they said they would. That side is an eight. Then there’s the other side, which is that I spent the quarter in rooms where we talked about the same AI decision and walked out of every one of them without making it, and that made me dread Mondays in a way I haven’t in six years. That side is a four. So, seven, maybe six and a half, and I don’t want the number to bury either half of it. If you read a seven and think we’re fine, you’ve missed the part that’s going to cost us next quarter, which is that nobody knows who decides.

Executive

    Vision

  • EOS Example

    The clearest one is the AI thing. We’ve had four conversations about what we build ourselves and what we buy, and every one of them ended with everybody nodding and nobody owning it. Then a couple of weeks later somebody has spun up a pilot anyway and we’re back arguing about the same thing. That’s not a people problem, honestly, it’s that we never wrote down where we’re going, so everyone fills the gap with their own version of it.

GTM

  • EOS Component

    Data

  • EOS Example

    We have dashboards everywhere and I still can’t tell you why fill rate moved in July. Marketing says it was the rate increase, sales says it was the outbound push, and both of them have a chart that says so. The data’s directionally right, I’m not saying it’s wrong — it’s that we can’t get from the chart to the cause, so we end up picking whichever story matches what we already wanted to do.

  • Avoided Conversation

    Whether we still run two motions. There’s the self-serve side for single offices and there’s a real sales motion for the groups, and the honest version is the single-office side isn’t paying for itself anymore, everybody knows it, and nobody wants to be the one who says kill it, because it’s somebody’s whole team. That’s the conversation. I’d rather have it in the room with everyone than in a spreadsheet three months from now.

  • Biggest Issue

    Handoffs. Marketing hands a lead to sales and it sits, sales hands an office to ops and the first shift doesn’t get filled, and every one of those is somewhere we lose an account without ever hearing about it.

Looking forward

  • Success Vision (90 days)

    Ninety days from now, the one I care about most is that there’s a single fill-rate number and nobody walks into the room with a second one. That’s the whole first thing. Past that — we have a charter for how we build with AI, one page, boring, and all six of us have signed it, so when somebody spins up a pilot we point at the page instead of relitigating it. The Gulf Coast group has run a full quarter on the new rate card and we actually know whether it worked, know it, not argue about it. GTM is down to one motion, which means the single-office funnel is either fixed or it’s off, and either of those beats what we’re doing now. Engineering is far enough through the worker app backlog that they’re asking us what’s next instead of us apologizing for the queue. And honestly the thing I’d have stopped: the Monday leadership meeting where we update each other for ninety minutes and identify nothing. If that meeting is half as long and twice as uncomfortable I’d take that on its own. Oh, and the headcount conversation — had it, not scheduled it.

  • Issues List

    Three or four for the list: who owns the AI decision, the single-office motion and whether we keep it, the headcount conversation before budget season, and if there’s room, how we run L10s now the room is twenty people.

Your rocks

  • Rock 1

    One fill-rate number, one owner, read at the top of every L10, and nobody brings a spreadsheet to sit next to it.

    • Why it matters

      Because every argument we had last quarter started with two numbers. If we can’t agree on what fill rate was, we can’t agree on what moved it, and we end up deciding by whoever is most certain that morning. That’s a bad way to run a marketplace.

    • What done looks like

      End of the quarter there’s one dashboard, one written definition, and one owner whose name is on it. We’ve read it in the L10 every week of the quarter and nobody has turned up with their own version. And when July’s question comes round again, we answer it in the room.

    • Who's involved

      Ops owns it, data builds it, and I’m the one who has to stop accepting a second number.

  • Rock 2

    Collapse GTM to one motion: one play, written down, that every rep runs from lead to first filled shift.

    • Why it matters

      Two motions is where our handoffs die. Every seam between them is somewhere a new office waits a week on something, and offices don’t complain, they just stop posting shifts. It’s also the cheapest efficiency we have — we don’t need more people, we need one path.

    • What done looks like

      The play is on one page, every rep has run it end to end at least twice, and we can watch a lead go from marketing to a filled shift without anyone forwarding an email. And we’ve decided about the single-office funnel out loud, either way.

    • Who's involved

      Sales and marketing together, ops for the last mile, and me to make the call on the funnel.
