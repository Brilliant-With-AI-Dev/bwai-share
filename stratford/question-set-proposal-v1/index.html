Stratford · Offsite Strategy Mirror

Proposal for review — not approved

Sub-questions for the Mirror.

Every participant answers privately, and the answers become one readout for the leaders before the offsite. The eight questions we have stay exactly as they are. This proposes what gets asked underneath them — plus two questions that have nowhere to hang.

Why I'm proposing this

The current eight do the hard part well. They get people talking honestly about how we work across units, and they separate what has to be decided from what can simply be discussed. I haven't changed a word of them. What they don't reach is most of what the shapers named as the reason for holding the offsite at all: the margin ambition and whether people privately believe it, what the credit and compensation model actually rewards, moving to AI-first, and the thing everyone in the business knows and nobody will say in the room. None of it comes up, so the readout can't tell the leaders where the organization stands on any of it.

So rather than rewrite the set, I've added underneath it — sub-questions that pull the specifics out of answers we're already getting, and two questions that have no parent, because margin and AI don't follow from anything currently asked. The test I applied to each one is whether a leader reading sixteen answers to it would learn something they could act on in the room. Worth a read now rather than a careful one later: the questions freeze before the 17th.

Adrian Head · Brilliant With AI

How to read this

  • The question in large type is the current wording, unchanged.
  • Indented items underneath are the proposed sub-questions.
  • Always fires every time. If thin fires only when an answer needs drawing out.
  • Two questions are marked New — they have no parent to sit under.
  • Full set runs about 25–30 minutes per participant.

Current reality

1 · Current wording

Where does "One Stratford" happen today? Where does the current structure help or hinder?

  • AlwaysThink of the last cross-unit opportunity you were part of. What happened when you needed people from another unit — or when you decided not to ask?
  • If thinWhat specifically made it work, or stopped it?

Why: the question as it stands invites a position. The sub-question asks for an incident, which is harder to argue with and gives the room real examples. "Or when you decided not to ask" matters — the person who never raised it is the one worth hearing from.

2 · Current wording

What would a client notice if they watched that from the outside?

Why: no sub-question. It turns the same problem outward and closes the stage cleanly. Adding to it would only slow the opening down.

Direction

3 · Current wording

Which choices do you understand as decided, recommended, or open? Where does your own view differ?

  • AlwaysLet me take six one at a time. For each, say decided, recommended, or open:
    • Who decides what across units, and who gets credit for shared work
    • Staying focused on mid-market, Canada first
    • Whether we name specific industries to focus on
    • How much of delivery is shared across the firm versus staying inside each practice
    • How many strategic commitments we take on
    • The revenue, margin and AI targets in the plan
  • AlwaysWhich one would you most want re-opened on the 17th?

Why: the highest-value addition on this page. The question currently collects an opinion about what is settled; the sub-question tests it, one item at a time, in language people can grade consistently. Sixteen people across six choices produces a map of where understanding is shared and where it is not — which is the thing the shapers asked to establish before the room opens.

Practical implications

4 · Current wording

If the firm commits to integrated delivery, what changes in your next ninety days? Think pursuits, staffing, and credit.

  • AlwaysName a live pursuit in the next quarter where integrated delivery would change what you do.
  • If thinWhat would you have to release, and what would you need back?

Why: the question already points at the right ground; the sub-question makes someone name something real rather than describe a pattern. One wording note for the group to decide: "if the firm commits" reopens a direction the shapers treat as settled in principle. The sub-questions work either way, but that clause is the single edit I'd make.

5 · Current wording

What's one thing that would have to be true for that to feel safe rather than risky?

  • AlwaysPut it in terms of targets, credit, or compensation: what would have to be true for you to release your strongest person to another unit's pursuit next quarter?
  • If thinWhat does the way we're measured today reward you for doing instead?

Why: "safe rather than risky" collects a feeling. The sub-question collects a condition. Shaper input warns that better collaboration is being expected without changing what people are measured and paid on — this is where that gap gets named, in each person's own words.

Judgment and doubts

6 · Current wording

What do you support, question, or oppose in the plan? What evidence might change your view?

  • AlwaysWhat's true, useful, and unlikely to be said out loud in that room?
  • If thinWhere does it feel like the load isn't shared evenly right now?

Why: a private conversation is the only instrument in this offsite that can collect the unsaid thing, and nothing currently asks for it. Shaper input already predicts roughly what it will be. Asking everyone both protects whoever raised it and shows how widely it is felt.

What the offsite must resolve

7 · Current wording

What must the room actually decide on the 17th, not just discuss? And what will you bring?

  • AlwaysUp to three — which are they?
  • AlwaysOn the 18th, what has to exist for this to be real — and what will you personally own?

Why: capping the list at three forces a ranking, which is what makes sixteen answers comparable. The second sub-question is the one a shaper asked for directly: what happens immediately after, and how decisions become work plans with named owners.

8 · Current wording

If the room can't agree, how should it be settled?

  • AlwaysThe CEO has said he'll make the call where the room splits. What do you need in order to get behind a decision that goes against your view?

Why: how a split gets settled is already answered, so the question as it stands implies the mechanism is still open. Reversed, it becomes the useful version: what people need in order to accept a call they lost. Worth deciding whether the parent stays as a warm-up or the sub-question replaces it.

The number

9 · New — no parent to sit under

Our 2026 plan targets a stretch gross-profit figure. Leadership believes a materially higher gross margin is achievable in each business line. Where do you land between those two numbers?

Both figures go into the live conversation. They are held off this page, not out of the question — asked without them, it cannot be answered.

  • AlwaysWhat would have to change in pricing, delivery or staffing for the higher number to be real in your area?
  • AlwaysWhich number you rely on today do you not trust, or would define differently?

Why: the most consequential topic in the preparation material, and nothing in the current set touches it. The two figures in circulation are not the same number and nobody is currently asked to notice. It returns a distribution rather than an anecdote, plus sixteen specific answers about what would have to change. The second sub-question goes at measures that get argued about instead of agreed — utilization being the live example.

AI

10 · New — no parent to sit under

Leadership wants AI used in every part of the business, starting now. Name one thing in your own work you could put AI into within ninety days.

  • AlwaysAnd one place you think it won't work yet.

Why: shaper input treats failing to work this out as one definition of a wasted offsite, and the current set never raises it. Naming the commitment inside the question means nobody has to guess whether AI-first is a firm decision. Asking for the limit as well as the use case keeps the answers honest.

Housekeeping

11 · New — one line at the end

May we attach your name to your answers in the readout, use them anonymously, or count them with everyone else's without quoting you?

Why: it settles how every quote in the readout can be presented. Without it, everything has to be treated as anonymous, which costs the leaders the ability to follow up with the person who said the useful thing.

Eight questions unchanged · twelve sub-questions that always fire · four that fire only when an answer needs drawing out · two new questions · one closing line. About 25–30 minutes per participant.
