Three things the ten questions don't ask.

I went through the participant Mirror against the three shaper voice notes and the two strategy documents, looking for anything the offsite needs that a participant would never be prompted to say.

Almost everything is already there — and in better shape than I'd have written it. What follows is the short list of what isn't, in the same format as the current set, so it can be dropped in or dropped entirely.

Proposed addition · Slide 3 of 6

What does everyone think is already decided?

Basic

Which parts of the strategy are already decided?

Better

Where do you think people disagree about what is still open?

Mirror · question and possible follow-ups

I'm going to read six choices from the plan. For each one, tell me whether you understand it as decided, a recommendation open to challenge, or genuinely open.

  • Who decides what across units, and who gets credit for shared work. Mid-market and Canada first. Whether we name specific industries.
  • How far integration goes. How many commitments we take on. The revenue, margin and AI targets in the plan.
  • And of those six, which one would you most want re-opened on the 17th?

Purpose: Establish where understanding of what is settled is shared and where it is not, before the room opens.

This is the one addition I'd fight for. It is also the least like the others — every current question opens with a scenario, and this one asks for a grading. The in-voice alternative is "Imagine someone says 'we've already decided that' — which choice would make you push back?", which sounds right but loses the comparison across sixteen people.

Proposed addition · Slide 4 of 6

What must actually be decided?

Basic

What decisions should come out of the offsite?

Better

What would you be disappointed to leave without deciding?

Mirror · question and possible follow-ups

Imagine the offsite ends and only three things actually got decided. Which three would you want them to be?

  • Would you rather the room settle one of those properly, or get through all three?
  • Which of them can't wait another quarter — and what happens if it does?
  • If the room splits and the CEO makes the call, what would you need to hear to get behind a decision that went against you?

Purpose: Separate what must be decided from what can be discussed, and produce a ranked list the room can open from.

Proposed addition · Slide 5 of 6

How may we use what someone says?

Basics page · alongside name, email and teams

May we attach your name to your answers in the readout, use them anonymously, or count them with everyone else's without quoting you?

  • Not a conversation question — it sits with the other basics, before the interview starts.
  • One shaper has already volunteered that either is fine for him. Nobody else has been asked.

Purpose: Settle how each person's words may be presented, so the readout can quote rather than treat everything as anonymous.

Over to you · Slide 6 of 6

What I need from you.

  1. A yes or no on each of the three. They're independent — cutting one doesn't affect the others.
  2. A call on the grading question's form. The comparable version breaks the scenario pattern; the in-voice version keeps it and loses the comparison. Slide 3 has both.
  3. One open judgement call. "If commitments get added, what comes off your plate" currently exists only as a possible follow-up under Q10. One shaper named it as his condition for backing the plan — worth promoting to a required question, or fine where it is.

Before we freeze. Happy to talk it through instead of writing it up.
