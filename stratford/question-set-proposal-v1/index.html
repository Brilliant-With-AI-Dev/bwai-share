Why I'm proposing this

The current eight do the hard part well. They get people talking honestly about how we work across units, and they separate what has to be decided from what can simply be discussed. I haven't changed a word of them. What they don't reach is most of what the shapers named as the reason for holding the offsite at all: the margin ambition and whether people privately believe it, what the credit and compensation model actually rewards, moving to AI-first, and the thing everyone in the business knows and nobody will say in the room. None of it comes up, so the readout can't tell the leaders where the organization stands on any of it.

So rather than rewrite the set, I've added underneath it — sub-questions that pull the specifics out of answers we're already getting, and two questions that have no parent, because margin and AI don't follow from anything currently asked. The test I applied to each one is whether a leader reading sixteen answers to it would learn something they could act on in the room.

Current reality

1 · Where does "One Stratford" happen today? Where does the current structure help or hinder? Wording unchanged

  • AlwaysThink of the last cross-unit opportunity you were part of. What happened when you needed people from another unit — or when you decided not to ask?
  • Follow-upWhat specifically made it work, or stopped it?

Why: asks for an incident rather than a position — and catches the person who never asked in the first place.

2 · What would a client notice if they watched that from the outside? No changes

Direction

3 · Which choices do you understand as decided, recommended, or open? Where does your own view differ? Wording unchanged

  • AlwaysLet me take six one at a time. For each, say decided, recommended, or open:
    • Who decides what across units, and who gets credit for shared work
    • Staying focused on mid-market, Canada first
    • Whether we name specific industries to focus on
    • How much of delivery is shared across the firm versus staying inside each practice
    • How many strategic commitments we take on
    • The revenue, margin and AI targets in the plan
  • AlwaysWhich one would you most want re-opened on the 17th?

Why: the most valuable addition here. The question currently collects an opinion about what is settled; this tests it, one item at a time, in language people grade consistently. Sixteen people across six choices produces a map of where understanding is shared and where it isn't — the thing the shapers asked to establish before the room opens.

Practical implications

4 · If the firm commits to integrated delivery, what changes in your next ninety days? Think pursuits, staffing, and credit. Wording unchanged

  • AlwaysName a live pursuit in the next quarter where integrated delivery would change what you do.
  • Follow-upWhat would you have to release, and what would you need back?

Why: makes someone name something real instead of describing a pattern. Separate note: "if the firm commits" is the one wording edit I'd argue for — it reopens a direction the shapers treat as settled.

5 · What's one thing that would have to be true for that to feel safe rather than risky? Wording unchanged

  • AlwaysPut it in terms of targets, credit, or compensation: what would have to be true for you to release your strongest person to another unit's pursuit next quarter?
  • Follow-upWhat does the way we're measured today reward you for doing instead?

Why: the question collects a feeling; this collects a condition.

Judgment and doubts

6 · What do you support, question, or oppose in the plan? What evidence might change your view? Wording unchanged

  • AlwaysWhat's true, useful, and unlikely to be said out loud in that room?
  • Follow-upWhere does it feel like the load isn't shared evenly right now?

Why: a private conversation is the only place the unsaid thing can be collected, and nothing currently asks for it.

What the offsite must resolve

7 · What must the room actually decide on the 17th, not just discuss? And what will you bring? Wording unchanged

  • AlwaysUp to three — which are they?
  • AlwaysOn the 18th, what has to exist for this to be real — and what will you personally own?

Why: capping at three forces a ranking, which is what makes sixteen answers comparable. The second is the morning-after question a shaper asked for directly.

8 · If the room can't agree, how should it be settled? Wording unchanged

  • AlwaysThe CEO has said he'll make the call where the room splits. What do you need in order to get behind a decision that goes against your view?

Why: how a split gets settled is already answered. Reversed, it asks what people need to accept a call they lost. Worth deciding whether the parent stays as a warm-up.

Three that are new

9 · New question — nothing currently asked leads to this

Our 2026 plan targets a stretch gross-profit figure. Leadership believes a materially higher gross margin is achievable in each business line. Where do you land between those two numbers?

Both figures go into the live conversation. They are held off this page, not out of the question — asked without them, it cannot be answered.

  • AlwaysWhat would have to change in pricing, delivery or staffing for the higher number to be real in your area?
  • AlwaysWhich number you rely on today do you not trust, or would define differently?

Why: the most consequential topic in the preparation material, and nothing in the current set touches it. The two figures in circulation are not the same number and nobody is asked to notice. Returns a distribution rather than an anecdote, plus sixteen specific answers about what would have to change.

10 · New question — nothing currently asked leads to this

Leadership wants AI used in every part of the business, starting now. Name one thing in your own work you could put AI into within ninety days.

  • AlwaysAnd one place you think it won't work yet.

Why: shaper input treats failing to work this out as one definition of a wasted offsite, and the current set never raises it. Asking for the limit as well as the use case keeps the answers honest.

11 · New closing line

May we attach your name to your answers in the readout, use them anonymously, or count them with everyone else's without quoting you?

Why: settles how every quote in the readout can be presented. Without it, everything has to be treated as anonymous.

What I need from you

  1. A yes to the set, or the ones you'd cut. Any of the sixteen can come out without breaking the others.
  2. A call on two wording questions I've deliberately left open — whether "if the firm commits" stays in question 4, and whether question 8's original stays as a warm-up now that its follow-up carries the weight.
  3. A view on the margin question. It needs both real figures to be answerable, and whether we attribute the higher one to a person or to leadership generally is your call.

Before we freeze the questions. If it's easier to talk it through than write it up, that works too.
