Proposal for review — not approved

Proposed Mirror questions.

Every participant answers these privately, and the answers become one readout for the leaders before the offsite. Eleven asks: three replace questions in the current set, five are new, three stay as they are. Each carries the reason it earns its place.

Current reality

1 · Replaces the current opener

Think of a cross-unit opportunity you were part of recently. What actually happened when you tried to bring in people from another unit?

What specifically made it work, or stopped it?

Why: the current opener asks where working as one firm happens today, which invites a position. This asks for an incident. A named pursuit that went well or badly is harder to argue with, and it gives the room real examples rather than sentiment.

2 · Unchanged

What would a client notice today if they watched two of our units work a single account?

Why: it turns the same problem outward, and it gives people a client-facing way to describe the cost of the current structure. Good warm-up, so it stays.

The map

3 · Replaces the decided-or-open question

I'll read six choices. For each, tell me whether you understand it as decided, a recommendation open to challenge, or genuinely open. Then: which one would you most want re-opened on the day?

Decision rights and credit across units · market and geographic focus · industry verticals · how far integration goes · how many commitments we take on · the growth, margin and AI targets.

Why: the biggest single change here. The current version asks for an opinion about what is settled; this tests it. Sixteen people across six choices produces a map of where understanding is shared and where it is not — which is exactly what the preparation material asks to establish before the room opens. Spoken aloud it still works, where a written grid would not.

Practical implications

4 · Same question, conditional removed

Name a live pursuit in the next quarter where working as one firm would change what you do. What would you have to release, and what would you need back?

Why: the current wording opens with "if the firm commits to integrated delivery," which reopens a direction the shapers treat as settled in principle — only the how is still live. Dropping the conditional keeps the tactical detail and closes the door on a debate that has already happened.

5 · New

What would have to be true in targets, credit, or compensation for you to release your strongest person to another unit's pursuit next quarter?

What does the current model reward you for doing instead?

Why: the preparation material warns that better collaboration is being expected without changing what people are measured and paid on. Nothing in the current set asks about that. This gets each person to name the condition in their own words, which is far more useful to the room than another statement of intent.

The number and the machine

6 · New

Our plan carries a stretch gross-profit target. Leadership input treats a materially higher margin as achievable in each business line. Where do you land — and what would have to change in pricing, delivery or staffing for the higher number to be real in your area?

Why: this is the most consequential item in the preparation material and the current set is silent on it. The two figures in circulation are not the same number, and nobody is currently asked to notice. It produces a distribution rather than an anecdote, plus sixteen specific answers about what would have to change.

7 · New

Which number you rely on today do you not trust, or would define differently?

Why: measures that get argued about instead of agreed — utilization being the live example — quietly block everything built on top of them. Cheap to ask, and it usually returns the most immediately fixable list in the set.

AI

8 · New

If we are AI-first starting now: name one thing in your own work you could put AI into within ninety days, and one place you think it will not work yet.

Why: the preparation material treats failing to work this out as one definition of a wasted offsite, and the current set never raises it. Asking both halves keeps it honest and returns sixteen candidate use cases the room can sort, rather than a debate about whether AI matters.

The unsaid, and the day after

9 · New

What is true, useful, and unlikely to be said out loud in that room?

Where does it feel like the load is not shared evenly right now?

Why: a private conversation is the only instrument in this offsite that can collect this, and the current set never asks for it. The preparation material already predicts roughly what will go unsaid; asking everyone both protects whoever raised it and shows how widely it is felt.

10 · Kept, and absorbs the tie-break question

What must be decided on the day rather than discussed — pick up to three — and what will you bring? Then: the morning after, what has to exist for this to be real, and what will you personally own?

The CEO will make the call where the room splits. What do you need in order to get behind a decision that goes against your view?

Why: the current set asks how a split should be settled, which is already answered and implies the mechanism is still open. Reversed, it becomes useful: what people need in order to accept a call they lost. The rest produces a ranked list of what has to close and a named commitment per person.

Housekeeping

11 · New, one line

May we attach your name to your answers in the readout, use them anonymously, or use them only in aggregate?

Why: it settles how every quote in the readout can be presented. Without it, everything has to be treated as anonymous, which costs the leaders the ability to follow up with the person who said the useful thing.
